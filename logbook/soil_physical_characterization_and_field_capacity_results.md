# Preliminary Soil Physical Characterization and Field-Capacity Measurements

**Experiment period:** June 2026  
**Exact experiment date:** Not recorded

## 1. Purpose

These measurements were made to characterize the soil used for capacitive soil-moisture sensor calibration. The work included:

1. collecting three approximately undisturbed soil cores;
2. separating fine soil from coarse fragments;
3. estimating coarse-fragment volume by water displacement;
4. calculating bulk-density-related quantities; and
5. estimating field capacity using a saturation-and-drainage pot test.

---

## 2. Soil-core sampling method

Three soil samples were collected using a cylindrical tube with the following **internal dimensions**:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Tube length | 25.7 cm |
| Tube internal diameter | 5.8 cm |
| Tube internal radius | 2.9 cm |

The tube was inserted vertically from the soil surface and pushed downward until it was completely filled. No additional manual compression was applied after collection.

The nominal internal volume of the tube was calculated as:

\[
V = \pi r^2 h
\]

\[
V = \pi \times (2.9\text{ cm})^2 \times 25.7\text{ cm}
\]

\[
V \approx 679.0\text{ cm}^3
\]

Therefore, each soil core represented a nominal volume of approximately:

- **679 cm³**, or
- **0.679 L**.

### Method limitation

Although no deliberate manual compression was applied, pushing a tube into soil can still cause some compaction, soil loss, or disturbance at the tube edges. The calculated volume therefore represents the tube's nominal internal volume.

---

## 3. Drying procedure

The soil was dried in an black outdoor oven.

The procedure was:

1. leave the soil in the black oven for approximately five days;
2. allow solar heating to raise the oven temperature naturally, typically to about 70 °C;
3. raise the temperature to approximately 110 °C for 2–3 hours;
4. weigh the soil;
5. heat it again at approximately 110 °C for another 2–3 hours; and
6. consider the soil dry when its mass no longer changed meaningfully.

In this report, **fine dry soil** means the oven-dry mass of all material that passed through the final approximately 2 mm sieve.

---

## 4. Sieving and coarse-fragment measurement

The intended final separation threshold was approximately 2 mm.

A household pasta sieve was also used during the first pass of Sample 3. Its openings were not uniform:

- most openings were approximately 3 mm;
- some were approximately 2 mm; and
- a small number were approximately 4 mm.

For Sample 3:

1. the first recorded coarse-fragment fraction was retained by the pasta sieve;
2. the remaining material was then passed through a sieve with approximately 2 mm openings;
3. the second recorded fraction was the additional material retained during that pass; and
4. the two coarse-fragment fractions were combined for the total.

Sample 1 was separated using only the approximately 2 mm sieve. Sample 2 was also separated, but its coarse-fragment volume was not recorded.

### Coarse-fragment volume method

Coarse-fragment volume was estimated by water displacement:

1. place 50 mL of water in a graduated container;
2. insert the coarse fragments;
3. record the increase in water level; and
4. treat the increase in millilitres as coarse-fragment volume in cubic centimetres.

Because trapped air, water absorption, irregular markings, and water adhering to the stones can affect the result, these volume measurements should be treated as approximate.

## 6. Derived soil-core quantities

### 6.1 Equations

Total dry core mass:

\[
m_{total} = m_{fine} + m_{stone}
\]

Whole-core dry bulk density:

\[
\rho_{b,whole} = \frac{m_{fine} + m_{stone}}{V_{core}}
\]

Coarse-fragment mass fraction:

\[
f_{stone,mass} =
\frac{m_{stone}}{m_{fine}+m_{stone}} \times 100
\]

Coarse-fragment volume fraction:

\[
f_{stone,volume} =
\frac{V_{stone}}{V_{core}} \times 100
\]

Fine-earth bulk density corrected for measured coarse-fragment volume:

\[
\rho_{b,fine} =
\frac{m_{fine}}{V_{core}-V_{stone}}
\]

The corrected fine-earth bulk density cannot be calculated for Sample 2 because its coarse-fragment volume was not measured.

### 6.2 Results by sample

| Sample | Whole-core dry bulk density | Coarse fragments by dry mass | Coarse fragments by core volume | Corrected fine-earth bulk density |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.105 g/cm³ | 14.80% | 10.01% | 1.046 g/cm³ |
| 2 | 1.082 g/cm³ | 16.46% | Not available | Not available |
| 3 | 1.268 g/cm³ | 20.47% | 17.38% | 1.221 g/cm³ |

### 6.3 Summary statistics

| Quantity | Result |
|---|---:|
| Mean fine dry-soil mass | 646.0 g |
| Mean coarse-fragment mass | 136.1 g |
| Mean total dry core mass | 782.1 g |

### Interpretation

The three cores were not identical. Sample 3 contained both the greatest coarse-fragment fraction and the highest whole-core bulk density.

The measured coarse-fragment content ranged from:

- **14.80% to 20.47% of dry core mass**; and
- **10.01% to 17.38% of core volume** for the two samples with displacement measurements.

This variability is relevant to capacitive soil-moisture sensing because coarse fragments reduce the fine-soil volume, alter local water storage, and can create air gaps near the sensor.

## 7. Field-capacity test

### 7.1 Method

Soil that had already passed through the approximately 2 mm sieve was placed in a pot with drainage holes.

Large stones were placed over the drainage holes so that:

- dry soil could not escape;
- water could drain freely; and
- the stones themselves were excluded from all reported soil masses.

The soil was watered until drainage was observed from beneath the pot. It was then left to drain for two days.

After drainage:

1. the wet soil was weighed;
2. the soil was oven dried; and
3. the dry soil was weighed.

The pot and drainage stones were excluded from both measurements.

### 7.2 Raw measurements

| Measurement | Mass |
|---|---:|
| Wet soil after two days of drainage | 527.8 g |
| Oven-dry soil | 360.6 g |

Mass of water remaining after drainage:

\[
m_w = 527.8 - 360.6
\]

\[
m_w = 167.2\text{ g}
\]

### 7.3 Gravimetric water content at operational field capacity

Gravimetric water content was calculated on a dry-mass basis:

\[
\theta_g = \frac{m_{wet}-m_{dry}}{m_{dry}}
\]

\[
\theta_g =
\frac{527.8-360.6}{360.6}
= 0.4637
\]

Therefore, the measured operational field capacity was:

\[
\boxed{\theta_g \approx 46.37\%}
\]

Equivalent expressions are:

- **0.4637 g of water per gram of dry soil**;
- **46.37 g of water per 100 g of dry soil**; and
- **167.2 g of retained water in 360.6 g of dry soil**.

---

## 8. Estimated volumetric water content at field capacity

Volumetric water content normally requires the bulk density of the soil in the same packed condition used during the water-retention test:

\[
\theta_v = \theta_g \frac{\rho_b}{\rho_w}
\]

where the density of water is approximately 1.0 g/cm³.

The packed soil volume in the pot was not measured. Therefore, volumetric field capacity **cannot be determined directly** from this test.

For context only, using the mean corrected fine-earth bulk density from Samples 1 and 3:

\[
\rho_{b,fine} \approx 1.133\text{ g/cm}^3
\]

gives:

\[
\theta_v \approx 0.4637 \times 1.133
\]

\[
\theta_v \approx 0.526
\]

or approximately:

\[
\boxed{52.6\%\text{ volumetric water content}}
\]

This is only a rough estimate because:

- the field-capacity soil was sieved and repacked in a pot;
- the actual pot bulk density was not measured;
- the core samples were collected in the field;
- the packing structure may therefore have differed substantially.

The estimated volumetric value should not be used as calibration ground truth unless the packed soil volume or pot bulk density is measured directly.

---

## 9. Terminology and interpretation

The result in this experiment is best described as **operational pot-drainage field capacity**:

> the water content remaining after the sieved soil was saturated and allowed to drain freely for two days.

It is not necessarily identical to a laboratory field-capacity measurement defined at a controlled matric potential, such as −10 kPa or −33 kPa.

The measured gravimetric field capacity of approximately 46.4% is relatively high. Before treating it as a fixed property of the soil, the test should ideally be repeated while also recording:

- dry soil volume in the pot;
- packed bulk density;
- soil height;
- pot dimensions;
- drainage duration;
- soil temperature;
- evaporation loss;
- and at least two independent replicates.

---

## 10. Relevance to the soil-moisture sensor experiment

These results provide several useful inputs for future sensor calibration:

1. The nominal sampled core volume is approximately **679 cm³**.
2. Whole-core dry bulk density varied from approximately **1.08 to 1.27 g/cm³**.
3. Coarse fragments represented approximately **15–20% of dry core mass**.
4. Measured coarse-fragment volume represented approximately **10–17% of core volume** where data were available.
5. The soil retained approximately **46.4% gravimetric water content** after saturation and two days of drainage.
6. A direct volumetric field-capacity value is still missing because the packed volume of the field-capacity sample was not recorded.
